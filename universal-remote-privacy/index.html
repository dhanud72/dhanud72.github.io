Universal Remote — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 September 2026

Universal Remote turns your phone into a remote for the TVs and speakers in your home. It has no account, no server and no database. It talks directly to your own devices over your home Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.

The devices you add, what you type on your TV and your pairing keys never leave your phone, except to reach the TV or speaker you are controlling. The only data that goes anywhere else is what Google's ad service needs to show a banner.

What we collect

Nothing. We operate no servers and hold no records about you. There is no sign-in, no profile, no analytics and no crash reporting. We cannot identify you, and we could not contact you unless you email us first.

Talking to your TVs and speakers

To find and control your devices, the app communicates only with devices on your own network or within Bluetooth range:

  • Finding devices. The Scan screen sends standard discovery messages on your local Wi-Fi network and lists the TVs that answer. It can also list nearby Bluetooth devices.
  • Pairing. Android TV, Google TV, Samsung and LG TVs ask you to approve the phone the first time. The app keeps the pairing key or token the TV issues in Android's encrypted storage on your phone, so it does not have to ask again.
  • Remote commands. Button presses, volume changes and app shortcuts are sent straight to the TV you chose.
  • Typing on the TV. Text you type in the app's TV keyboard is sent only to your TV, to fill in the field on its screen. It is not stored or sent anywhere else. If you use Search on TV, the TV opens that search in the chosen app, such as YouTube, which handles it under its own privacy policy.

None of this passes through us. Your devices' names, network addresses and pairing details stay on your phone.

What the app stores on your phone

So your remotes are ready next time, the app keeps these in its own storage on your phone:

  • the devices you add: their name, room, type, network address or Bluetooth address
  • your scenes, button layouts and settings, such as theme and language
  • the pairing keys and tokens described above, in Android's encrypted storage

None of it is uploaded or backed up by us. If you use Export backup, the app creates a file with your devices, scenes and layouts (never your pairing keys) and hands it to Android's share sheet. Where that file goes is your choice.

Advertising

The app shows a banner supplied by Google AdMob. To do that, Google's ad software collects information from the device, which typically includes:

  • the device's advertising ID
  • IP address, and an approximate location derived from it
  • device and app information, such as model, OS version and app version
  • interactions with the ad, such as whether it was seen or tapped

This is collected by Google, not by us. We receive only anonymous, aggregated totals of how many ads were shown. Google's handling of the data is described in the Google Privacy & Terms and the AdMob help centre.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in Settings → Google → Ads on your device.

Permissions the app requests

  • INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, ACCESS_WIFI_STATE and CHANGE_WIFI_MULTICAST_STATE — to find TVs on your Wi-Fi and send them commands, and for the ad banner.
  • BLUETOOTH_SCAN and BLUETOOTH_CONNECT ("Nearby devices"), and on Android 11 and older BLUETOOTH, BLUETOOTH_ADMIN and location — only to find and connect to Bluetooth speakers and headphones. Older versions of Android require the location permission for any Bluetooth scan; the app never reads or uses your location. The app asks for these when you scan for devices, and refusing them is fine if you only use Wi-Fi TVs.
  • VIBRATE — for the short vibration on each button press, which you can turn off in Settings.
  • AD_ID, Android's ad-services permissions (ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, _ATTRIBUTION, _TOPICS), WAKE_LOCK and FOREGROUND_SERVICE — added by Google's ad software and the Android libraries it uses, for their own housekeeping. The app itself runs no foreground service.

That is the complete list. The app requests no microphone, no camera, no contacts and no storage permission. The Voice button asks your TV to start its own voice search; the app does not record audio.

Deleting your data

Everything the app keeps is on your phone. Remove a device from its options menu, or remove all of the app's data by clearing its storage in Android settings or uninstalling it. There is nothing held anywhere else for us to delete. To stop a TV trusting the phone, remove it from the TV's own list of paired or connected devices.

Children

Universal Remote is not directed at children and is not intended for use by anyone under 13.
